# T435 S1 - 80386DX Manual and Model Ledger

## Status and boundary

This is block 4 of T435 S1. It covers every current 80386 successful-retirement
instruction form and context: compatible forms, 80386 additions, 16/32-bit
operand and address attributes, real/protected/VM86 successful contexts,
privilege paths, and legal prefixes/repetitions. The CPU profile's instruction
core is the Intel386 DX. A narrower external bus variant is transaction work,
not a replacement timing source for this CPU program.

## FLAGS image and restore contract (T499 S12)

In real and virtual-8086 mode the 80386 defines bit 15 of the 16-bit FLAGS
image as zero; bits 14--12 reflect their last loaded values, subject to the
manual's POPF/IRET privilege rules. This is Manual-L3 from I386-PRM-1986,
FLAGS and POPF/IRET descriptions. Core therefore uses one profile-owned
defined-field mask for both the published PUSHF/interrupt image and POPF/IRET
load value, rather than retaining a separate high-bit mirror.

## Timing interpretation and ownership boundary

Chapter 17 supplies an exact scalar, an explicit `r/m` pair, or a formula for
every successful form. Its baseline is prefetched/decoded instruction, aligned
memory, no bus wait state, no local-bus HOLD request, and no exception. `pm=`
selects the protected-mode result; `n` is repetitions, and `m` counts specified
components of the next instruction. The manual also supplies data-dependent
early-out multiply formulae and path-specific protected-mode control-transfer
rows.

These are L3 CPU-program inputs. READY/wait states, HOLD/BUSLOCK arbitration,
device/port response, instruction-fetch starvation, cache/board policy, and
time spent halted or waiting for BUSY are external transaction/device inputs.
Fault/interrupt delivery and task-switch exception paths are not successful
retirement; their own manual tables remain later event-timing work. VM86 does
not erase the instruction table: successful VM86 forms use their specified
real/protected/IOPL path, while VM86 exception delivery stays outside this
ledger.

## Coverage result

Intel supplies a direct L3 result for every successful 80386DX form: exact,
labelled context, or executable formula. Therefore no 80386 instruction needs
an L2 model or an L1 fallback. Future implementation must mechanically map all
grouped form/context keys to the timing program and must keep the stated
external/event boundary; it may not substitute an anonymous one-tick fallback.

This completes the four individual S1 evidence blocks. The next permitted work
is the owner review and four-profile reconciliation/closure decision, not a
silent runtime change.
