# M5 T153 S1: Design CPU And FPU Profiles

## Objective

Define frozen, per-machine CPU and FPU profiles plus the correct FPU escape
contract needed after T152 proved that MS-DOS `MEM` executes `FNINIT` (`DB
E3`). Produce the bounded implementation tasks; do not change execution
semantics in this design task.

## Profile Contract

- `core_machine_config` receives immutable CPU profiles `8086`, `80186`,
  `80286`, and `80386`, plus independent FPU profiles `none`, `8087`, `80287`,
  and `80387`. `core_machine` owns the frozen selections after construction.
- The default NXVM session is `CPU=80386, FPU=none`, preserving the currently
  intended integer default without claiming complete i386 compatibility.
- CPU and FPU profiles are independent. A CPU selection does not imply a
  coprocessor, and no session may hot-switch either profile.
- `SESSION OPEN --cpu <model> --fpu <model>` becomes the future NXVM creation
  surface; `SESSION LIST` displays both selections. Until a present FPU model
  is implemented, the product must clearly reject user requests for
  `8087`/`80287`/`80387` rather than create a deceptive session.
- `8086` is a strict instruction-architecture profile, not an assertion that
  the surrounding VM is an IBM PC/XT hardware clone. VM device/profile choice
  remains separate.

## CPU Decode Contract

- Every accepted opcode form has a `minimum_cpu` classification. Forms selected
  by ModRM fields have their own classification; a primary opcode table entry
  does not certify all sub-forms.
- Prefixes are classified too. `8086` rejects operand/address-size prefixes
  (`66`/`67`), FS/GS (`64`/`65`), and all other post-8086 forms through `#UD`
  before operand decode or guest-visible side effects.
- The `0F` escape is rejected in `8086` except documented `POP CS`, which gets
  an explicit positive probe. Later `0F` extensions may not leak through just
  because a handler exists.
- The census distinguishes 8086 base forms, 80186 additions, 80286 additions,
  80386 additions, and post-80386 encodings. `CPUID`, `RDMSR`, `WRMSR`,
  `WBINVD`, and `RSM` are post-80386 and must remain `#UD` in every planned
  CPU profile.
- The unconditional legacy `i386(n)` macro is removed only once the complete
  form census is available. It must not become scattered handler-local checks.

## FPU Escape Contract

- `D8` through `DF` are FPU escape opcode spaces, not direct `#UD` opcodes.
  They dispatch by escape opcode plus ModRM form through a core-owned FPU
  dispatcher.
- FPU state, metadata, dispatcher, trace, diagnostic data, fetch/prefix/ModRM
  handling, and callbacks follow the existing `core/machine` CPU conventions.
  FPU state is owned by its `core_machine`; no `vfpu`, `vcpu`, `vram`, `vport`,
  current-object, thread-local, or process-global facade is permitted.
- With `FPU=none`, a legal FPU escape consumes its complete encoding and
  continues. It raises `#NM` only when CR0 `EM` or `TS` requires it. It must
  not report `#UD` solely because no FPU is installed.
- `FWAIT` raises `#NM` only when `TS && MP`; its current TS-only behavior and
  incorrect `#NM` diagnostic text require repair.
- A legal form for a configured but not-yet-implemented present FPU must reach
  a distinct unsupported-FPU diagnostic path, never pretend it is `#UD`.
  T157 prevents such profiles from being selected through the product UI until
  their model/state/operations are implemented.
- The FPU form table owns `minimum_cpu` and `minimum_fpu` metadata. It may not
  inherit later instructions from a reference table without an explicit local
  classification.

## Non-Goals

- No no-op workaround, complete present-FPU arithmetic/state emulation,
  generic i386 capability claim, protected-mode or paging completion,
  IBM PC/XT hardware profile, CPU/FPU hot switch, VDM/DOS-module work, raw
  trace file, or default Bochx dependency.

## Verification And Research Boundary

- Positive and negative owned microprobes cover profile construction, CPU form
  availability, FPU escape consumption, CR0 `EM`/`TS` behavior, `FWAIT`, and
  diagnostic execution points. T156's owner-local `MEM` gate must pass
  `FNINIT` and report its next outcome.
- Local Bochs 2.6-compat may be consulted only as a bounded behavior reference.
  It is never copied, compiled into the product, or made a default dependency.

## Implementation Breakdown

1. **T154:** add CPU/FPU enums and frozen ownership, create the complete
   opcode-form `minimum_cpu`/`minimum_fpu` metadata, and add construction
   probes. No user-visible selection yet.
2. **T155:** centralize CPU profile gating, remove unconditional `i386(n)`,
   and prove strict 8086 rejection of 80186/80286/80386 forms with positive and
   negative probes.
3. **T156:** implement FPU ESC dispatch for `D8`--`DF`, `FPU=none`, and FWAIT
   CR0 `EM`/`TS`/`MP` behavior; default to `80386+none`; prove `MEM` advances
   past `FNINIT` and reports its next result.
4. **T157:** implement `SESSION OPEN --cpu <model> --fpu <model>` and profile
   display in `SESSION LIST`; reject unimplemented present-FPU selections and
   prohibit hot switching.
5. **T158:** close the static census: every form is classified or illegal,
   `i386(n)` is absent, FPU ESC spaces have no direct `UndefinedOpcode` route,
   and GCC, Console, debugger, FDD boot, and task-artifact gates pass.

The work remains M5. M6 stays blocked, and present FPU profiles are not
claimed usable before a later state/operation implementation sequence.
